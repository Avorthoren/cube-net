This app should visualize and let user work with cube networks: 2D polygons (with no self-intersections) that can be
folded and wrap a cube without overlapping. For example, for a cube with a side of 1 unit any cube network, obviously,
will have an area of 6 square units. Everywhere after we consider edge of the cube to be equal to 1 unit (unless
we talk about pixels), so that we can describe relative sizes of other elements compared to cube size.
Simplest example of cube network would be 4 squares connected
"in a line" (1-2-6-5) and two more squares attached on both sides (4 and 3), kinda like a cross. Like this:
```
 1
423
 6
 5
```

Colors that look somewhat alike are deliberately assigned to opposite faces (1-6, 2-5, 3-4): opposite faces never
share an edge or a vertex, so those colors never appear together in one dashed edge or one vertex circle, while
colors of faces that meet in the same cube vertex are very different.
And that assignment should be stable:
i.e. each time new project it started, we have the same assignment. So, every time we start a new project, face

faces using numbers I used for default cube net.
Color assigned to a face should be used in 3 ways:
1. A face should be colored using its color and a white color. How? Each face should be divided into 50 equal rectangles,
   with width of 1/10 unit and height 1/5 unit. Each rectangle should be divided diagonally into two triangles,
   one of which should have face's color, and another should be white. When we look at default cube net, each of 6 faces
   (6 big squares) should have their small rectangles divided by the diagonal that goes from the bottom left
   to the upper right corner. And upper triangle should have face's color, while the bottom one should be white.
   The pattern belongs to the face (it's defined in the face's own coordinates): whenever the face or any of its parts
   is moved/rotated, the pattern moves/rotates together with it. That's the whole purpose of the pattern: so that user
   can easily see where each part of the cube net "came from" and how it's oriented.
2. The edge between two faces should have some small width, and it should be a "dashed line", that alternates colors
   assigned two faces it touches. More specifically, dashes have constant length measured in units (1/20 unit), so that
   a whole edge of the cube has 10 dashes of each of 2 colors, and a shorter segment (part of an edge, or a cut made
   by user) just has proportionally fewer dashes. The dash pattern is "anchored" to the position along the cube's
   segment, so that both images (counterparts) of the same cut show exactly the same pattern. This includes outer
   perimeter of the cube net. For example, edge A_2 - E_2 of the outer perimeter of the default cube net corresponds
   to the edge between two faces of the cube (5-th and 4-th faces), hence, it has to consist of dashes of two colors
   (colors assigned to 5-th and 4-th faces of the cube). Later user will be able to make other "cuts", so the outer
   perimeter of the cube net may have not only segments that represents edges of the cube, but other segments, that
   don't separate two faces of the cube. Those kinds of segments have to be colored using dashes of color of the cube's
   face they lay inside and white color. To sum up: segments that separate faces of the cube, have to always be
   "present", "visible" no matter if they are part of outer perimeter (for example, A_2 - E2) of the cube net or not
   (for example, C_1 - D_1), while additional cuts made by user have to be "visible" only when they are on the outer
   perimeter of the cube net.
3. Vertices of cube's faces should be circles of some small radius. They should be divided into 3 equal sectors,
   each one of the color of respective face (since in each vertex of the cube 3 faces meet). Cube have 8 vertices,
   but when it's "unfolded" into cube net some vertices will be represented more than once. For example, default
   cube net has 14 "images" of 8 cube vertices (example below). Later user will be able to make other "cuts", so
   the outer perimeter of the cube net may have not only vertices that represent vertices of the cube, but other
   vertices. Some of them may lay inside the edge between two cube's faces - they have to be divided into 2 equal
   sectors with colors of respective cube's faces this edge separates. Some of them may lay inside the cube's face -
   they will be colored using 1 color of respective cube's face. "Visible" vertices can only be on the outer perimeter
   of the cube net.
   Vertex circles are centered at the "real" location of the vertex (unlike cuts, which are drawn inside the face).
   Ideally sectors should somehow "point" towards respective cube net faces (and maybe even be of different sizes),
   but how exactly - is to be decided later. For now: equal sectors.
   If you aren't able to infer proper coloring for vertices - ask me.

Now to the main feature: cube net transformations.
Obviously, to "flatten" a cube (make a cube net) one must do some cuts in the cube's surface. For default cube net
we have cuts along edges: AC, BD, EH, FG, AE, BF, AB (7 out of 12 edges are cut). In any cube net by "cut" we consider
a segment of the outer perimeter of the cube net (and each cut will be represented as two segments of the
outer perimeter of the cube net: A_1 - C_1 and A_2 - C_1, A_1 - B_1 and A_3 - B_3, etc).
Imagine that each point of the cut has its own 1D coordinate system (each cut's point can be represented
with number from [0, 1] segment). I.e. basically, we consider cuts being directed. And those coordinate systems "match"
for cuts that denoted with same letters, because such cuts will be "glued" point-to-point when cube net is folded
into the cube. For example, if for AB edge of the cube we may assign coordinate 0 to vertex A and coordinate 1
to point B, then its default cube net will have coordinate 0 for A_1, A_2 and A_3, while B_1, B_2 and B_3 will have
coordinate 1. Keep in mind, that vertex of the cube is part of multiple cuts, hence multiple different coordinates
may be assigned to it (one for each cut it's part of).
Now, the statement is, that if we cut cube net into 2 pieces (each piece will have outer perimeter, part of
which was present before we cut it off, because it was the outer perimeter of the cube net, and part of which is a new
cut), rearrange them and glue in another way (so that we glue by matching cuts, matching coordinates of all glued
points) - we get another cube net. Of course, we additionally have to make sure that there is no overlapping, and
resulting shape is a connected domain.
For example, if we cut corner of 1st face of the cube, we might get something like:
```
                  A_1 ----- x_1
                   |         |
                   |        y_1 --- z_1
                   |        1        |
                   |                 |
                   |                 |
A_2 ------------- C_1 ------------- D_1 ------- z_3 - B_2
 |                 |                 |                 |
 |                 |                 |                 |
 |        4        |        2        |        3        |
 |                 |                 |                 |
 |                 |                 |                 |
E_2 ------------- H_1 ------------- G_1 ------------- F_2
                   |                 |
                   |                 |
                   |        6        |
                   |                 |
                   |                 |
                  E_1 ------------- F_1
                   |                 |
                   |                 |
                   |        5        |
                   |                 |
                   |                 |
                  A_3 ----- x_2 --- B_3
                             |       |
                            y_2 --- z_2
```
Here we made two new cuts: xy, yz.
We glued part xB of the existing cut AB, so another part Ax of this cut still exist, which we can treat as new cut.
Cut BD was divided into Bz and zD - we can treat them as new cuts.
And this one is tricky, because when we were making the cut (xyz) it's not obvious from the first glance that we have
to "divide" D_1 - B_2 with a new point (in this case z_3), but we have to carefully analyze each cut to not miss
anything.
By the way, in this case point x will have colors 1 and 5 (because it's on the edge between faces 1 and 5),
point y will have color 1 (because it's inside edge 1) and point z will have colors 1 and 3.

Now the question is: how to allow user to make new cuts.
First let's describe it logically. I see two steps in the cutting process:
1. Describe the "line" (in the example above it consists of two segments) that divides cube net in two parts.
   It should start on the outer perimeter and end on the outer perimeter. And nowhere in the middle should it touch
   the outer perimeter. I.e. it should be strictly inside the cube net except for the starting and ending points.
2. Gluing those two part in another way.
Now from the UI perspective. First step from picking some point on some of the existing cuts (outer edges of the cube
net perimeter). It should be followed by picking 1 to N other points (let's limit N to 50), and as soon as we pick
point on the outer perimeter - the cut line ends "logically" (i.e. we can't add points past the last point).
I see it like this (we forget for measuring tool for now):
Initially we are in the DEFAULT state, in which:
1. If we hover the mouse over any cut - it "highlights".
2. If we click on any cut (remember, cuts have some width, so that user can clearly see their coloring
   and click on them) - the starting point of the cut line is created. All vertices and segments of the new cut line
   that we are creating should follow the same coloring rules described earlier with one exception: they have to be
   slightly different, kinda "highlighted", so that user can see which one are the old ones.
   Important: as soon as an endpoint of the new cut line (the first one or the last one) is created on some segment
   Xi - Yj of the outer perimeter, its counterpart Xk - Ym has to be divided by the image of the same cube's point
   immediately (that's the z_3 case from the example above), not later when we glue. Otherwise in CUT_EDITING state
   user would not be able to click the segment they actually want to glue, because it would not exist yet.
   These counterpart images are shown as regular perimeter vertices (and follow the "highlighted" coloring, since they
   are part of the new cut line process). Undoing creation of the endpoint removes its counterpart image as well.
As soon as first point of a new cut line created, we are in a slightly different state (CUTTING):
1. Application should show segments that connect i-th and (i+1)-th point of the new cut line, and segment that connects
   the last point with current mouse location.
   While SHIFT is held, that segment is restricted to horizontal or vertical (whichever is closer to the mouse), and it
   changes at once when SHIFT is pressed or released. Whatever it snaps to has to keep it that way: a vertex of the outer
   perimeter only if it's exactly on its line, a segment of the outer perimeter or a fold line at the point where its
   line crosses them.
2. As soon as user clicks inside the cube net application should check if segment from the last point to current mouse
   location has intersection with other segments of the new cut line or the outer perimeter. If so - current segment
   should just become black for 200 ms. If there is no intersection - new point of new cut line should be created.
   Keep in mind, that if only have 1 point (the first one) and we click on the same segment of the outer perimeter
   the first point is - potential new segment of the new cut line would intersect the outer perimeter (because all its
   points are there), so that's a rejection case.
   More rules about endpoints of the cut line:
   - Endpoints may be located on existing vertices of the outer perimeter (including vertices of the cube).
   - Cut line may start and end on the same segment of the outer perimeter (if it has intermediate points).
   - Cut line can't start and end at the same point of the cube net.
   - Cut line MAY start and end at two different images of the same point of the cube's surface (for example, A_1 and
     A_3). Such cut makes it impossible to glue anything (every candidate segment has its counterpart in the same half),
     but we don't forbid it: user will see why it doesn't work and will revert it.
   Helping user to hit exact places (a click counts as being "on" something inside its hitbox, in this order):
   - inside the circle of a vertex of the outer perimeter: the cut line starts/ends exactly at that vertex;
   - inside the hitbox of a segment of the outer perimeter: it starts/ends at the point of that segment closest to the
     click;
   - inside the hitbox of a fold line (same total width as the hitbox of a segment of the outer perimeter, but centered
     on the line, as the line itself is drawn): the new point of the cut line is the point of that fold line closest to
     the click. Doesn't apply to the very first point of the cut line, which has to be on the outer perimeter.
     The fold line highlights while the mouse is in its hitbox, like a segment of the outer perimeter does, whenever
     that means a new or a moved point would land on it.
As it was said earlier, as soon as we create another point on the outer perimeter of the cube net (in addition to the
very first one, from which this new cut started) - we transition to the next state (CUT_EDITING).
Vertices of the outer perimeter (counterpart images included) can't be clicked in this state, so they don't highlight
when hovered. The exception: while the first/last point of the cut line is dragged, the vertex it would go to is
highlighted (that's never a counterpart image: the point can't go to those).
1. Here in the middle of every segment of the new cut line should be a small hollow circle (just a thin outline, with
   transparent inside), click on which should create new point of the new cut line.
   Sizes: the hollow circles have 0.6 of the vertex radius, and the small dots of the line (the points where it
   crosses cube edges, the images of its ends) half of it; but none of them is ever smaller across than 140% of the
   edges' thickness (they keep that size when their share of the vertex radius would be less). The vertex circles
   themselves are at least 140% of the edges' thickness across too: the vertex radius can't be set below 0.7 times the
   edge thickness, and if the edges are made thicker, the vertex radius grows with them, just as much as needed (and
   stays when they're made thinner again).
2. SHIFT+click on any point of the new cut line (except for first and last one) should delete that point (when the mouse
   is released, if it has hardly moved: SHIFT+drag is a move, see below). CTRL+click does the same: while CTRL is held
   over such a point the mouse pointer is a red x; the point is deleted when the mouse button is released on it (CTRL
   never drags; released elsewhere, nothing happens). But there is
   a catch: consider 2 consecutive segments P1 - P2 and P2 - P3 of the new cut line, and user tries to delete P2.
   If segment P1-P3 intersects any other segment of the new cut line or the outer perimeter (i.e. violates the rule
   "new cut line should be strictly inside the cube net"), then for 200 ms we hide segments P1 - P2, P2 - P3 and show

3. User should be able to click-and-drag any point of the new cut line. The first and the last point should move only
   along the outer perimeter: their position is defined by the point closest to the current mouse location, and they
   walk from segment to segment when they pass a vertex (without releasing the mouse). If the mouse is inside the circle
   of a vertex of the outer perimeter, they go to that vertex. Other points of the new cut line should never be on the
   outer perimeter: if such point is released while the mouse is inside a hitbox of the outer perimeter (a segment or a
   vertex), the movement is rejected. If such point gets inside the hitbox of a fold line, it goes to the point of that
   fold line closest to the mouse, and it stays on that fold line (moving along it) until the mouse leaves that hitbox.
   While SHIFT is held, the segment from the previous point to the dragged one is restricted to horizontal or vertical
   (the first point has no previous one, so it moves as usual), with the same snapping rules as while cutting. The last
   point stays on the outer perimeter: it goes to the point of the perimeter closest to the mouse that is straight from
   the previous point.
   As soon as user releases the mouse click application
   should check if any segment of the new cut line that has moved point (two segments if that's an inner point of the
   cut line, or one if that's the first/last point) has intersection with any other segment of the new cut line or
   the outer perimeter. If so - moved segment that breaks the rules should become black for 200 ms and moved point
   should reverse its position back.
   When the first/last point is dragged, its counterpart image (see z_3 case) moves accordingly along the counterpart
   segment, so that both images always represent the same point of the cube's surface.
   While the ruler is not active, nothing is being dragged, and the mouse is over a segment of the outer perimeter (in
   its hitbox), every segment of the outer perimeter whose counterpart is in the same half of the cube net is drawn thin
   and black, showing that clicking on it can't rearrange anything. Segments here include the divisions made by the
   first/last points and their images: only the affected parts of a cut change. The segments of the new cut line
   itself are not affected.
   If the segment under the mouse isn't one of those (clicking on it would rearrange the halves), its counterpart
   blinks instead: regular and invisible in equal durations, 6 changes per second, so that user can see which segment
   would be glued with it. The vertices at its ends stay visible.
4. If user clicks on any segment of the outer perimeter (not including its vertices) that should indicate the intent
   of rearranging two parts cube net was divided in such a way, that this chosen segment is glued with its counterpart.
   Because of the way we defined the cut if there is segment of outer perimeter Xi - Yj, then there always is exactly
   one more segment Xk - Ym, where at least one of these inequalities holds: i != k, j != m. I.e. for any cut between
   points X and Y on the cube surface there should be exactly two segments of the cube net perimeter with those two
   letters X and Y, and we call those segments counterparts of each other.
   If the counterpart of the clicked segment belongs to the same half of the cube net as the clicked segment - such
   rearrangement is impossible: clicked segment becomes black for 200 ms, and nothing else happens.
   Clicks on segments of the new cut line itself are ignored (both of its "sides" coincide at this moment, so gluing
   them would mean nothing).
So, as soon as user clicks on the segment of the outer perimeter we should do that rearrangement (moving half
of the cube net that has clicked segment counterpart, but not moving half that has clicked segment), and transition
to the next state (CUT_APPROVING):
1. Application should still show the segment we are about to glue.
2. It should check if new outer perimeter has any self-intersections, and if so - it should color intersecting segments
   of new perimeter in black.
   What exactly counts as "self-intersection" must be defined very carefully. The real criterion is: interiors of
   cube net faces must not overlap. Touching is NOT a self-intersection and doesn't forbid the cut:
   - two parts of the cube net may touch in a point;
   - two segments of the perimeter may lie on each other (fully or partially), if the cube net is on opposite sides
     of them (that's the reason cuts and their hitboxes are drawn inside faces: so that user can still click on cuts
     of either of two touching cube net faces);
   - a vertex may touch a segment of the perimeter from outside.
   Self-intersection examples: two segments cross each other; two segments lie on each other with the cube net on
   the same side of them; a vertex "pokes" through a segment into the cube net.
3. In the middle of the segment user clicked on there should be a round button with a check mark (constant size in
   pixels). If there are no intersections, it is green, and clicking it (or the shortcut CTRL+ENTER) ends the process of
   cutting the cube net, getting us in the DEFAULT state. If there are intersections, it is grey, shows an "x" instead of the
   check mark, and clicking it does the same as CTRL+z: it undoes the rearrangement. CTRL+ENTER does nothing then.
   Nothing but this button can be clicked in this state, so cuts and vertices of the outer perimeter don't highlight
   when hovered.

Now let's describe actions that should be saved and be reversible (and redoable):
1. Creating new point of new cut line.
2. Deleting point of new cut line.
3. Moving point of the new line cut (as soon as user releases the click - that's a new position to save).
4. Moving from CUT_EDITING to CUT_APPROVING state.
5. Accepting new cut line (moving from CUT_APPROVING to DEFAULT state).
N last actions should be saved into the project (so they should be available even after we restart the app).
Let N be 1000. When we already have N actions saved and do the next one - the oldest one should be forgotten.
CTRL+z should reverse the last one, CTRL+y should redo the last reversed. But if after reversing the action we make
a new action regularly - we should forget all possible actions that could have been redone. I.e. something like
circular buffer would be good here.
Few exceptions:
1. If the last added/redone actions had type #5 - reverting should try to revert not one but two last actions.
   Why try? - Because if we add more than N actions, then the oldest one can happen to be type #5. And if user
   then reverts 999 actions, and presses CTRL+Z again we will not be able to revert to more, so just 1 in this case.
2. Consider action of type #4 that made an invalid cube net (i.e., user wasn't allowed to approve this cut line and
   move further, so the only possible option from there was to revert the action). If user reverts such action - we
   should "forget" about it, i.e. user shouldn't be able to redo it. Maybe, this feature would make single circular
   buffer, and we would need to use something like deque for main history and stack for reversion history.
CTRL+y always redoes exactly one action (even if CTRL+z reverted two of them: after reverting #5 and #4 together,
first CTRL+y redoes #4 and we get to CUT_APPROVING, the second one redoes #5).
The redo stack is saved into the project too.
There is no separate "cancel cut line" feature: user just reverts actions with CTRL+z.
We don't save anything related to ruler instrument. And if its activated - it doesn't interrupt main functions:
we still draw existing cuts and the new cut line we are creating right now, bounding rectangle, etc. But while ruler
is active, clicks on the sheet create ruler points (and only them), not points of the cut line. Also: if we are in the
middle of creating new cut line, then we don't show segments that connects last created point of the cut line with
current mouse location when ruler instrument is active. As soon as we deactivate ruler instrument - we get back to
main functions.

Few more notes:
1. We don't need vertices labels be visible to user.
2. If we do cuts properly showing all cut vertices everywhere they can be visible (point z shown in 3 places
   in the example above, including z_3), then we don't need to think about some coordinate system attached to every
   segment, because gluing rule would just become "gluing same vertices together". For that we'd have to
   create additional vertex on the cube's edge if cut intersects the cube's edge.
3. Each vertex of each cut and folding edge of the cube net should "know" original cube faces it's related to.
   This way it's easy to know the colors of the vertex - colors of all those cube faces. Since we should always draw
   initial cube edges (even if they are not cuts), and sice we should make cuts (segments of the cut line) not go
   from one cube's face to another (i.e. if cut line intersects cube's edge, then that intersection point should be
   the boundary between two consecutive cuts) - all vertices of the cube net face will always have exactly one common
   cube's face, which should define the color of the cube net face. As for cuts: each cut should have colors of faces
   that its ends are related to: there will be either two such faces or one (which means, it should be that color
   and white)
4. Cuts should be drawn in such a way, that they are inside the cube net. Same goes for their hitboxes. I.e. to click
   on the cut user have to hold the mouse inside the cube net face it bounds (not anywhere inside the face obviously,
   just close to the cut). So the center-line of the cut hitbox should not be the geometric cut itself (in which case
   half of the hitbox would be outside cube net face it bounds), but it should be inside cube net face.
5. As described above in CUT_EDITING state user can select specific cut to be glued back again. But after user approves
   the cut (and its valid) application should check if adjacent cuts from two glued parts "match" (i.e. they are defined
   by same vertices on the surface of the cube, and they overlap point-by-point. If there are such cuts - they should be
   glued automatically.
   For example, there is cut line on the cube's surface that consists of consecutive points P, Q, R, S, T.
   Now consider that related cube net has consecutive cuts P1 - Q2, Q2 - R1, R1 - S1, S1 - T3 and "somewhere else"
   consecutive cuts P3 - Q1, Q1 - R2, R2 - S2, S2 - T1. If after creating new cut line in CUT_EDITING state user clicks
   on, let's say, Q2 - R1, and halves of the cube net get rearranged, and there no overlapping
   (i.e. self-intersections), and user approves the cut line - application should glue Q2 - R1 with Q1 - R2. Then it
   should search in both "directions" starting from cut Q - R. If now (after rearranging) P1 matches P3 - it should
   glue P1 - Q2 to P3 - Q1. If S1 matches S2 - it should glue R1 - S1 to R2 - S2. When searching in specific direction
   it should stop as soon as it finds "next" cut that can't be glued. I.e. if T3 doesn't match T1 - we should NOT
   look past point T.
6. Redundant points should disappear. After the cut is approved (and auto-glued), some points on the surface of the
   cube may become meaningless: a point that is not a vertex of the cube and is not a branching point of cuts, i.e.
   at all its images the outer perimeter just goes "straight through" it, and both adjacent cuts have the same colors
   (e.g. point x, if later parts Ax and xB of edge AB become one cut again). Or a point that became internal (not on
   the outer perimeter at all). Such points are removed (from all their images), and adjacent cuts are merged.
7. Some facts we agreed on:
   - Vertices of the cube are always on the outer perimeter of the cube net (only 270 degrees of "paper" meet there,
     so it can never be an internal point of a flat net). Hence a cut line never passes through a vertex of the cube.
   - Every rearrangement is a rotation by a multiple of 90 degrees plus a translation.
   - Application stores models of both the cube's surface (points on it, cuts) and of the cube net (images of those
     points and cuts on the plane). Labels (A_1, x_2, ...) are shown next to the vertices when the user switches them
     on (Settings -> Preferences); that's also how several user-made vertices on the same cube's face are told apart. They stay in
     the app, off by default in releases (session logs too).
   - Snapping: points of the cut line snap to vertices and segments of the outer perimeter and to fold lines, and
     SHIFT restricts segments (see above). Nothing snaps to the unit grid, and ruler points don't snap.

Settings
In the menu bar, to the right of Edit, there's "Settings": it opens a panel on the right side of the window, over the
sheet (the sheet doesn't move, and the rest of it works as usual); clicking "Settings" again hides it. These are
settings of the whole application, not of a project. The panel takes at most a third of the screen's width; its tabs
are listed horizontally, and scroll if they don't fit. The panel opens on the tab the user looked at last (even in an
earlier run of the application), and the application starts with the panel open if it was open when the user left it.
The very first time the application starts, the panel is open.
A small button in the panel's upper right corner (a double chevron pointing right, not a cross, which would look like
closing the application) hides the panel, like clicking "Settings" again. Every change is applied and saved at once, and shows on the
sheet at once.
1. Preferences:
   - Language (first): "Language" and a drop-down list with "English" and "Русский" to the right of it ("Язык" when
     it's Russian). The list stays where it is when the language changes. Every text of the application is shown in
     the chosen language (Qt's own too: standard buttons, file dialogs), at once, and the choice is remembered;
   - Show vertex labels;
   - Auto-start logging: when it's on, logging (the session log) starts by itself when the application starts.
     Switching it doesn't start or stop logging now: the button above "Logs" on the instruments panel does that (a
     red dot, "Start logging", while not logging; a red square in a frame, "Stop logging", while logging);
   - Blink when target area achieved (on by default): if it's off, the
     "Bounds" button doesn't blink on success, it's just green while the area is below the target;
   - Maximum zoom (pixels per unit, see the zoom section).
2. Style. Every section starts with its name and a "Restore default" button to the right of it, which restores the
   defaults of just that section after the user confirms it.
   - Faces colors: 6 lines, the k-th for face k: a 10x10 square of its colour, then '#' and an input for the colour as
     RGB hex digits.
   - Faces tiling: the number of columns and rows each face is divided into (now 10 x 5), integers from 1 to 30.
   - Cuts and folds: number of dashes of each colour along a whole cube edge (1 to 30, now 10), and their thickness
     in pixels (1 to 15, now 5). Hovered elements are drawn 1.5 times as thick, as before.
   - Vertices: radius of the vertex circles in pixels (from 0.7 times the edge thickness to 30, now 8).
   - Grid: a white 10x10 square crossed in its middle by a vertical and a horizontal grid line, the colour of the
     grid, and the thickness of its lines in pixels (0 to 5; 0: no grid).

Mouse pointer (while the ruler is not active; it tells the user what a click would do):
1. DEFAULT: over a cut (or a vertex of the outer perimeter, where a cut can start too) - scissors.
2. CUTTING: inside the cube net (or in a vertex circle, which can end the line) - scissors.
3. CUT_EDITING: over a point of the new cut line that can be dragged - a pencil; over the circle in the middle of a
   segment (a new point there) - a thick green plus with a black outline, which clicks at its upper left inner
   corner (where the bars meet); over an inner point while CTRL is held (deleting it) - a red x (the same plus, turned by 45 degrees, red); over a segment of the outer perimeter that can be chosen for gluing (not a thin black
   one) - a tube of glue. While a point is dragged, the pencil stays.
4. CUT_APPROVING: over the round button - the pointing hand.
   The round button and the hollow circles in the middle of the new cut line's segments act like buttons: pressing
   one only shows it pressed (the round button pushed in, a circle all black, the same size); it acts when the mouse
   button is released on it. Moved off it before that, it doesn't act (and looks pressed again if the mouse comes
   back). The button looks raised (lit from above, with a soft
   shadow), and its tooltip says "Approve", or "Cancel" when it shows the "x".
Elsewhere it's the usual arrow; while the sheet is being dragged, the closed hand.
